# Live mode — plan

A market day the vendor is trading *right now*: the storefront leads with the market it is
standing in, the day's menu carries per-dish availability, and the vendor closes the day when
they pack up. Wireframe: `tmp/live-mode.png` (its logo and *bientôt épuisé* tag are not the
design).

Nothing built yet. `ItemMarkedAsSoldOut` exists in the domain with no read model, route or UI
(`NEXT_BEHAVIOURS.md`); `inProgress` already derives live-ness from schedule times and badges
**En cours** on the customer market card.

## Decisions

Settled by grilling. Do not re-litigate without a reason.

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Open is derived, close is an event.** Live = `inProgress && !closed`. No `MarketDayOpened` | The failure modes are asymmetric: a forgotten *open* tap kills the feature during the exact window it exists for, a forgotten *close* leaves today's behaviour. Close is also the trigger the later three-outcomes rating hangs off |
| 2 | **`MarketDayClosed` is an early `endTime`** — `notYetEnded` learns about it, ~~the day drops out of upcoming for vendor and customer~~ the day leaves the **customer's** view (revised by decision 10 — the vendor keeps it until `endTime` so it can be reopened), `inProgress` goes false, the storefront falls back to the normal page | One rule reused. Without it a vendor who sold out at 11h30 leaves a card reading *8h–13h · En cours* until 13h — worse than having no close at all. No bespoke *c'est terminé* screen: the next-market card already answers *when can I get this*. Two markets in one day works with no special case — closing the morning market promotes the evening one, which goes live on its own `startTime` |
| 3 | **Split the query.** `FindNextMarketDay(vendorId)` → one occurrence + dishes + availability + live/closed. `FindUpcomingMarketDays` sheds its menu join and becomes a pure calendar read | Live mode's state is day-scoped; joining sold-out across a 56-day horizon puts a third range read into a window join to serve one row. Days 2–5 already carry a menu list that is structurally always empty (decision 7 of `MENU-DU-JOUR-PLAN.md` leaves no UI to plan beyond the next market). **Contradicts that plan's decision 5** — menus rode on `MarketDayOccurrence` when a menu was the only day-scoped data. Also kills the frontend's `days().find(c => !c.absent)` scan at `next-menu-card.ts:38` |
| 4 | **No `FindCurrentMarketDay`** | Decision 1 makes *current* mean *next, after its `startTime`* — a second query would range over the same data and return the same row |
| 5 | **The menu editor narrows to the next market day** | It is only reachable from the dashboard card, so it always opened the next day in practice. A hand-typed URL for any other day falls into the existing *Ce marché n'est plus programmé* branch. Slice 4 declined a single-day GET because the editor prefilled from `dishes` on the upcoming payload; decision 3 removes that payload, and *plan later in time* becomes the trigger for `FindMarketDay(marketId, date)` |
| 6 | **Sold-out is reversible: `ItemMarkedAsAvailable` mirrors `ItemMarkedAsSoldOut`** — per-item, timestamped, not a whole-set replace | A vendor who taps the wrong row one-handed in the rain otherwise tells every approaching customer a dish is gone, fixable only by re-saving the menu — which nobody will discover. **Deliberately does not extend ADR 0047** (a market day's menu is set whole): the menu is a plan edited once in a sitting, availability is a stream of moments during service. A set loses the `time` the event already carries, and makes two taps on market wifi last-write-wins. Replay yields a per-dish availability timeline the waste-watch work can read. `ItemAlreadySoldOutError` stays as an idempotence guard and gains a mirror |
| 7 | **The undo does not sit next to the action.** Sold-out dishes leave the vendor's active list and collect in an *Épuisé* group at the bottom; restore is a tap inside that group | A toggle serves *found another tray* and causes *wrong row*. Marking stays one fast tap on a full-width row; the row visibly moving is the receipt, so a misfire is obvious instantly with no toast, timer or snackbar to build. The gradient is right — the active list shrinks as service goes on, the épuisés group grows. Rejected: undo toast (misses the later scenario), long-press/swipe (undiscoverable), confirm dialog (friction on the action that must be fastest). The **customer** list does not re-sort — sold-out greys in place, per the wireframe; shuffling rows under a reader's thumb is worse than a dead row |
| 8 | **The customer page polls the existing storefront endpoint every 60s while live and visible, and re-fetches on `visibilitychange` → visible.** *maj. il y a X min* counts from the last **successful** fetch. Repeated failures leave the page live with a stale timestamp | Sold-out marks that need a manual reload are worth nothing — the customer who matters is five minutes' walk away. Focus-refresh is the one that earns its keep: the real behaviour is open, walk, pocket the phone, pull it out at the stall. Rejected SSE/WebSocket — a long-lived connection and a projection→client bridge to save 30s for one pilot vendor. Reusing the storefront endpoint re-sends the carte each poll (a few kB of strings); split out a `/live` route when a real payload makes it measurable. The timestamp is the failure disclosure, not decoration — a covered market hall with bad 4G is the likely case, and a pulsing **EN DIRECT** over twenty-minute-old data is the lie the feature exists to avoid. Dropping the layout on a flaky tunnel would be a bigger lie than a stale menu; the customer can read *il y a 12 min* and judge |
| 9 | **Per-variant sold-out stays deferred.** A variant dish is one row on the vendor's live screen and greys whole on the customer's | The pilot vendor has no variant dishes, so it is a case that does not yet exist. ADR 0033's tolerant reader means adding `variantName` to `ItemMarkedAsSoldOut` later is additive and needs no version bump — so pre-adding it is scaffolding. Deferring also defers variant identity: `name` is the identity today and `ReviseItem` edits the whole set, so a per-variant mark could be orphaned by a rename; that lands with the ordering slice, which needs stable variant identity anyway. **Trigger: the first pilot vendor who puts a variant dish on a live menu** — the *popular format goes first* case is the normal one for such a dish, not an edge, so this flips from deferred to blocking the day it appears |
| 10 | **Two vendor routes, not one mode-switching screen.** `/dashboard/menus/:marketId/:date` stays the planner; live gets its own route. The dashboard card is the switch — it links to the editor before `startTime` and to live during. The live screen carries a discreet *Modifier le menu* link, and **Close** sits at the bottom, below the épuisés group | Same day, same dish list, an hour apart, opposite verbs — one screen flipping mode on a clock rather than on anything the vendor did is decision 7's footgun one rung up. The card is already a doorway (`MENU-DU-JOUR-PLAN.md` decision 7) and decision 3 gives it `live`, so the vendor never chooses. The editor must stay reachable during the market — *j'ai apporté une plaque de plus* is real, `setMenu` only guards past days, and adding a dish mid-market already clears sold-out for dropped items (`market-day.ts:26`). Close at the end of the page is the furthest point from the rows tapped all morning |
| 11 | **Close is reversible: `MarketDayClosed` / `MarketDayReopened`**, per-moment and timestamped like decision 6. `FindNextMarketDay` keeps a closed day until its `endTime` carrying `closed: true` — the vendor card renders *Terminé · Rouvrir*, the customer storefront reads closed as ended. `FindCustomerStorefrontHandler` drops that date+market from the *Prochains marchés* list it composes | A vendor who closes at 10h30 and then serves a straggler has otherwise ended their day publicly with no way back — and under decision 2 as first written, no query returned the day at all, so nothing could reach it. One query, two readings. The list filter matters: decision 3 made `FindUpcomingMarketDays` a pure calendar read with no market-day join, so without it the closed market still lists as *8h–13h* today and a customer walks over at 12h. No reopen guard — after `endTime` the day is unreachable through any UI, and a hand-crafted call would reopen something `notYetEnded` still drops |
| 12 | **Live requires a non-empty menu**: `inProgress && !closed && dishes.length > 0`. No menu → today's behaviour, the normal storefront with the **En cours** badge | The likely pilot morning is a vendor who was loading the van at 6h and never planned. Without this the storefront replaces its hero with *on est au marché* over an empty *Au menu aujourd'hui* — a stall advertising no food, seen by every customer that morning, and the vendor who forgot to plan is the one who won't notice. Degrades both ways on its own: planning from the stall at 8h30 brings the takeover up on the next poll, so there is never a *go live* button; everything selling out keeps the menu non-empty so the page stays live with every row greyed, which is what a customer needs and the loudest hint to close. Accepted cost: a vendor can be at a market with no takeover and nothing tells them why — fixed on the vendor side, where the card during a live market with no menu reads *Vous êtes au marché · aucun plat au menu* rather than the planner's neutral prompt |
| 13 | **Two terms, not three. *En cours* = the market is running (schedule truth, `inProgress`, unchanged). *En direct* = the vendor is broadcasting today's menu (decision 12's trigger).** While live, *Prochains marchés* starts at the **next** market | Decision 12 made these genuinely two states — a live market with no menu is honestly *en cours* and not *en direct* — and *en direct* is French broadcast language, which is what the page is doing. The repeat rule in `MENU-DU-JOUR-PLAN.md` (a list silently starting at the second market reads as a bug) held for a modest card at the top; it does not survive a full-page takeover, where the day would appear twice — once as the hero, once as a card badged *En cours* beneath it. Merges with decision 11 into one rule: **the upcoming list excludes the day the page is currently featuring or has closed.** Normal mode keeps repeating the promoted day, unchanged. `NEXT_BEHAVIOURS.md`'s *open market day · close market day* is retired by decision 1 |
| 14 | **Live state extends `market-day-view` — one row per `(vendor, market, date)`, gaining `soldOutItemIds` and `closed`.** The store keeps intent methods (`markSoldOut` · `markAvailable` · `close` · `reopen`) so the projection stays a pass-through and the array append happens in SQL and in the in-memory twin. `setMenu` intersects `sold_out` with the new `itemIds`, mirroring `market-day.ts:26` | Post-decision-3 there is exactly one consumer of live state and it needs the menu in the same breath — nobody reads availability without the dishes it qualifies. Two rows keyed identically and always fetched together is a join paid on every poll to express a distinction no reader makes. Read models being disposable argues *for* the cheap shape: if it is wrong it costs a migration and a rebuild, which this codebase does routinely. The drift on that last rule is nearly harmless (a stale id isn't in `itemIds`, so the catalogue join drops it) except when the vendor re-adds the dish the same day — the flustered-mid-market case the feature exists for. **`menusFor(vendorId, from, to)` narrows to a point lookup and wants a name like `forDay`** — reversing decision 4 of `MENU-DU-JOUR-PLAN.md`, which had itself reversed an earlier point-lookup choice. Third decision live mode overturns |
| 14a | **The plan/outcome split is the waste-watch seam, not this one.** When the three-outcomes work lands it gets its own view — retrospective, keyed by dish across months, reading past days — built from the same `ItemMarkedAsSoldOut` / `MarketDayClosed` events | Conceptually the menu is a *plan* and availability is *what happened during service*, and that distinction is real. It just isn't a distinction any current reader makes: splitting now shapes a read model around a query that does not exist. The events are the durable record; the view that expresses the split gets built when something asks the question |
| 15 | **Occurrence assembly gets one home before either query is written** — expand recurrence → apply absences → join facts → apply temporal predicates | Decision 3 splits one handler into two, so the assembly currently inside `FindUpcomingMarketDaysHandler` would get two. That drift already bit once: `notYetEnded` and `parisWallClock` had to be hoisted out of the storefront handler in slice 5a. `MarketDayOccurrence` is a query DTO with no owner. **The structural invariant this all sits on:** *which day is next* and *is it running now* are time-driven, not event-driven — no event fires when a market starts or ends — which is why recurrence is expanded at query time and why a denormalised `next-market-day-view` (or a fully-projected storefront row) is not available: it would go stale at `endTime` with nothing to nudge it, and this system has no scheduler, only event-driven polling subscriptions |
| 16 | **All four commands require the day to be *today*** — same shape as the existing `MarketDayInThePastError`, derivable from `_today` alone | *Require the day to be live* is not available: live needs `startTime`/`endTime` from the calendar, and slice 4 refused to read calendar state from the market-day write path off an eventually consistent read model. Closes `MENU-DU-JOUR-PLAN.md`'s known issue (marking next Saturday's dish sold out). Unreachable through the UI anyway — the live screen only exists during live mode — so it guards against a hand-crafted call. Deliberately unguarded: marking sold out at 6h, before the market opens; legal, harmless, arguably honest. **Known limit:** a market crossing midnight cannot work — taps after 00h would be rejected against the previous date. Pre-existing, since `endTime` is a `LocalTime` with no next-day concept, but live mode is the first feature that would notice |
| 17 | **The carte moves to its own `/carte` page permanently, not just while live.** `app.routes.ts` becomes a parent holding the resolver, with `''` → `StorefrontPage` and `'carte'` → `CartePage` as children. Ships as its **own slice, before the takeover** | Deletes a conditional instead of adding one — the *demote the carte while live* rule existed only to stop two row-lists stacking, and a permanent split means live mode never has to know the carte exists. Two genuinely different jobs: home is *should I go*, carte is *what can they make*. Better SSR surface too — a real indexable page per vendor with dish names in it, and `storefront-metadata.ts` is already there to feed. One fetch serves both since children inherit the parent resolve; `RenderMode.Server` on `**` already covers the path. **The poll (decision 8) moves to the parent**, gated on live and running regardless of which child renders — on the page it would refetch-with-a-flicker or show stale prices on return from `/carte`. **The risk is discovery, not architecture:** the carte is the differentiator `MARKET_MIAM.md` builds the pitch on and now costs a tap, so it needs a visible *Notre carte* entry on the home page, not a footer link |
| 18 | **Slices: (1) sold-out end to end, (2) close, (3) *en direct* takeover** — with the `/carte` page independent, before slice 3 | Slice 1 has **no takeover**: sold-out greyed in place on the *Prochain marché* card that already exists is the whole customer-side value at no hero-redesign cost, and it is the honest test of whether vendors tap it at all — if they don't, slices 2 and 3 shouldn't be built. The query split (decision 3) waits for slice 3 at near-zero rework, because `dishesByDay` already range-scans the `MarketDayView` rows that decision 14 adds `soldOutItemIds` to — availability arrives in the read already being done. Slice 1 does need one non-obvious piece: **`inProgress` moves onto the occurrence.** It lives in `find-customer-storefront.handler.ts:51` on the customer DTO only, and the vendor's dashboard card needs it to switch from planner to live screen — decision 15's refactor arriving early, in its small form |
| 19 | **`PUT /market-days/:marketId/:date/dishes/:itemId/availability`, body `{ soldOut: boolean }`** — one idempotent route behind both commands; void response like every other command route | The menu route's whole-set `PUT` followed from *the menu is a set*, which decision 6 rejected for availability. Rejected `POST`+`DELETE /sold-out` (two routes, and `DELETE` reads as *delete the dish*) and a whole-set `PUT` (re-imports the last-write-wins problem). **Knowingly breaks the house one-route-one-command style**: a vendor's phone retrying on bad market signal must be safe, and `{soldOut: true}` twice is, where `POST` twice raises `ItemAlreadySoldOutError` and reads to the UI as a failure |
| 20 | **Accessibility ships in slice 1, not a polish pass.** `aria-live="polite"` on the **dish list only**; availability is text and never colour (drop `DISPONIBLE` with `BIENTÔT ÉPUISÉ` — the default state needs no badge); a sold-out row stays tappable; the pulse dot is `aria-hidden` and its animation gated on `prefers-reduced-motion`. On the vendor side, **focus moves deliberately when a tapped row leaves the active list**, and the move is announced | The page mutates with no user action and the change is a dish becoming unavailable — the highest-stakes silent mutation on it. Scoping the live region to the section rather than the list makes *maj. il y a X min* re-announce every minute forever, which turns the feature into one people switch off. Decision 7's row-leaves-the-list is the sharp edge: it destroys the focused element, so a vendor on a keyboard or switch control loses their place on every tap and the épuisés group becomes a trap. Cheap now, expensive to retrofit |
| 21 | **All liveness stays server-side; the client only ever re-asks.** The customer frontend gets `Clock` from `@market-miam/common` as an Angular provider, and *maj. il y a X min* is a wall-clock delta `now − lastSuccessfulFetch` | Letting the browser flip into live mode at `now >= startTime` computes liveness in the *browser's* timezone — a customer checking from London sees the Lyon market closed for another hour. Slice 4 paid for that lesson once with `TZ=Europe/Paris` on the API service, and the customer frontend has no timezone handling at all (ISO strings split against a lookup table, no `Intl`, no `registerLocaleData`). A tick counter for the label breaks where it matters: a phone in a pocket suspends timers, so a tab backgrounded twenty minutes would read *il y a 2 min* — the exact lie decision 8 exists to prevent. Reusing `Clock` keeps ADR 0013's *now enters in exactly one way* true repo-wide instead of adding a second time abstraction for one label, and makes the label testable by fixing the clock rather than faking timers |
| 22 | **`concatMap` on the availability effect**, not the `switchMap` the menu save uses | `market-day.effects.ts:37` uses `switchMap`, which *cancels* the in-flight request when the next arrives — correct for one whole-set save per screen, silently fatal for rapid taps: three dishes in five seconds and only the last survives, with no error shown while the vendor believes all three landed. `concatMap` queues instead, and because each request completes before the next loads the aggregate, the `ConcurrencyError`→409 from concurrent appends to one stream cannot arise from a single client. Decision 19 pays off here: per-item routes give per-item failure, so a tap that fails on bad signal reverts its own row visibly while the others stand — a whole-set `PUT` would fail them together and leave the UI guessing. The optimistic patch means the queue drains behind the UI, so a slow request never blocks the vendor. **Named limit, not solved: two devices still conflict** — a phone and a tablet at one stall can 409 each other. Retry-on-409 is the fix if it ever appears; building it now is infrastructure for a scenario the pilot cannot produce |
| 23 | **A second demo vendor that is always live**, scheduled `00:00–23:59` every weekday, with **menus scripted a year ahead** by a one-off `PUT` loop. Sold-out is demonstrated live during a call, not seeded | Live mode is otherwise invisible outside real market hours — undemoable to a prospect, unverifiable in production, unseeable in local dev. A second vendor keeps `demo.marketmiam.fr` showing the vitrine pitch while the live pitch has its own subdomain, so both are reachable at once. `00:00–23:59` stays inside one day, clearing decision 16's midnight limit. **Decision 12 is what forces the script**: always-live needs a menu every single day, nothing will plan it, there is no scheduler (decision 15), and a demo-vendor special case in the query is `?preview=live` wearing a hat — a code path whose purpose is to render a false claim about a vendor being at a market, on the page whose credibility the feature rests on. `SetMarketDayMenu` guards past days but has no future limit, and the editor's next-day narrowing (decision 5) is a UI constraint not an API one, so the script also proves *plan later in time* works before the UI does. **Decision 16 makes sold-out unseedable** — no script can pre-mark a row for a day that isn't today — which is fine once the demo is something you *drive*: dashboard on one screen, storefront on the other, tap, watch it grey out inside 60s. That is a better demo than a static badge, and the one moment decision 8's polling delay becomes a selling point. The website keeps a screenshot for the épuisé state |
| 24 | **No new spans or span attributes. The deferred stuck-subscription alert moves from evidence-gated into slice 1** | Every failure here is silent — nothing throws when a projection stalls or a poll dies, and the page serves stale data under a confident *En direct*, converting a neutral page into a misleading one. The events are already the analytics record (`ItemMarkedAsSoldOut` carries its `time`; waste-watch reads events, not telemetry), so `O11Y-PLAN.md`'s `menu.item_count` precedent is not extended — Honeycomb is for operations, not for business questions the log answers forever. The customer-side failure needs no instrumentation because decision 8 already ships it: there is no frontend telemetry (ADR 0026 bootstraps in `apps/api` only) and RUM for one pilot vendor is a new pillar, while *maj. il y a X min* is read by the only person whose judgement matters. `NEXT_BEHAVIOURS.md:35` parks the alert as evidence-gated with the design done — live mode **is** the evidence: a lagging projection used to mean a menu showed up late, now it means a sold-out dish stays on sale |
| 25 | **Occurrences sort by `(date, startTime ?? '00:00', marketId)`** — fixed in slice 1 | `find-upcoming-market-days.handler.ts:44` sorts by date alone and `postgres-market-schedule.views.ts:18` returns schedules `ORDER BY seq`, so a vendor with a morning and an evening market gets them in **registration order**. Already wrong today (the storefront lists them backwards); live mode makes it structural — `FindNextMarketDay` takes the first occurrence, so the hero would name the evening market and its pitch while the vendor stands in Croix-Rousse, then fail to switch when they move. The fallback matches `hasStarted` at `market-day-clock.ts:33`, which already treats a missing `startTime` as the start of the day, so nothing new is decided about untimed markets; `marketId` last makes the order total rather than stable-by-accident. One line, and every decision saying *the first occurrence* assumes it |

## ADR

[0049 — A market day opens by the clock, closes by hand](adr/0049-market-day-opens-by-the-clock-closes-by-hand.md),
for decision 1. An asymmetric lifecycle in an event-sourced domain is exactly what a later
contributor "corrects", and the reason — asymmetric failure modes, not oversight — is invisible
from the code.

Considered and skipped, because read models, routes and queries here are cheap to reverse and the
rationale above is enough: the query split (3), live state on the menu row (14), the idempotent
availability route (19), server-only liveness (21).

## Named limits

Accepted deliberately; none block a slice.

- **No presence guarantee.** *On est au marché* is inferred from the schedule, so a vendor whose
  van broke down reads as trading until `endTime` or until they close (ADR 0049).
- **A market crossing midnight cannot work** — decision 16. Pre-existing: `endTime` is a
  `LocalTime` with no next-day concept.
- **Two devices can 409 each other** — decision 22. One vendor, one phone is the real case.
- **Overlapping markets.** The schedule model permits two markets whose hours overlap; one stall
  cannot be at both, and first-by-start-time wins (decision 25). A data-entry mistake, not a rule
  to write.
- **Per-variant sold-out** — decision 9, with its trigger recorded there.
