Ordering

Customers order and pay online from a live market day's menu and collect at the stall with a code. Ordering is in the only package, 50 € HT a month, with no commission; Stripe's fees on a vendor's payments are the vendor's. The plan is docs/ORDERING-PLAN.md. Agreed rules

All four agreed on 24 October 2026, and amended on 31 October.

R1

Nothing is recorded before Stripe holds the card

Placing an order only opens a Checkout page. Our first event is the webhook saying the card is held. Anonymous traffic never touches the append lock (ADR 0028). An abandoned checkout exists only in Stripe's dashboard.

One exception: a customer cancelling an order that still waits for the vendor, with the token from its status link. Cancels can't outnumber held orders.

Saturday noon is about 10 writes a second. The measured ceiling is about 165 a second at a 1.7 ms round trip to the database (finding M6).

R2

Processors call Stripe; webhooks record the outcome

A processor reacts to one of our events, calls Stripe, and writes nothing. Stripe's outcomes enter our log only through webhooks: checkout, capture, cancellation, refund. The append lock never waits on Stripe.

Each request uses the order or registration ID as its idempotency key, so a retry cannot act twice. Stripe keeps keys for at least 24 hours. A refused account creation is recorded through a command (step 1); a refused capture still needs a path (Q5).

Account webhooks carry no state. Each records a notice, and a processor fetches the account before dispatching a command: that command's write waits on Stripe, the append lock does not (step 1).

R3

Stripe onboarding is prefilled from the mentions légales

We create the vendor's Stripe account with what we already hold: business type, legal name, address, SIRET, TVA number, contacts and legal representative from the mentions légales, name and URL from the storefront, the caterer activity code. Stripe asks only for the rest: IBAN, the representative's date of birth, home address and ID, a société's owners. The vendor confirms what we prefilled and can edit it before accepting Stripe's terms. A vendor who already has a Stripe account can reuse its verified details instead (networked onboarding), and those override ours.

Prefilling is one shot. Once the first onboarding link exists, we can no longer read or write the account's verification details; later changes go through the vendor's Express Dashboard (step 1). The live client fills in their mentions légales first.

French platforms must use account tokens. The vendor app sends the prefill from the vendor's browser to Stripe with Stripe.js, loaded only from js.stripe.com, and only the single-use token reaches our API; the account is created from it. Stripe.js also collects fraud signals from the vendor's device, a line for the vendor-space privacy policy (C2). Read Account tokens.

R4

A storefront takes orders only while its Stripe account can take payments

Every order checks the account: only active card payments let it through. Until then, and whenever the account stops taking payments, the storefront stays up and ordering pauses (step 1).

Publication no longer waits for Stripe (31 October 2026). A storefront goes live with its legal identity and terms, so Stripe's website check has a site to read, and the vendor connects Stripe afterwards. Setup order: mentions légales, publish, Stripe. The live client connects Stripe like any vendor.

Step 1: the payment account

Q1, decided 31 October 2026. We store what our rules decide on and read nothing else from Stripe: the vendor sees Stripe's details on Stripe's own screens, through the onboarding link or the Express Dashboard login link. When Stripe is down, only those links fail.

Codes go in the events, messages in the vendor app. The webhook adapter translates Stripe's statuses into our codes, so the domain never sees Stripe's words. Accounts v2 sends thin events, so every account webhook means the same thing: something changed. The processor fetches the account, naming the merchant configuration and the requirements in include, and dispatches a command; a repeated webhook records nothing. It reads notices in log order, and ADR 0036 lets one instance commit each, so a stale fetch never lands after a fresher one.

The create call uses the registration ID as its idempotency key and puts the vendor ID in the account's metadata, so the confirming notice finds the vendor. The registration screen says to get in touch if nothing happens within 10 minutes; we then check Stripe's dashboard and resend the notice, possible for 15 days. A stalled processor trips the stuck-subscription alert first. Payouts stay in Stripe, and the account on erasure waits for the erasure work. Read Account event types, How events work, Handle verification, Verification updates.

When the account stops taking payments (Q11, decided 31 October 2026), the storefront stays up and takes no new orders. Held orders are refused and their holds released. An accepted order whose capture Stripe then refuses raises an alert on the vendor's screen (Q5). The vendor sees a banner on the live screen, and a push when the vendor app is installed on the Home Screen: iPadOS 16.4 or later, with a web app manifest and a service worker the app doesn't have yet.

Our legal identity and Stripe's (Q9, decided 31 October 2026). We can't read the account's identity once the first onboarding link exists, so Stripe does the checking: the account's business URL is the storefront, and Stripe compares the site, mentions légales included, with the account. A Stripe account in another entity's name breaks the vendor terms (article 9). When the vendor changes their mentions légales, the vendor app prompts them to update Stripe through an Express Dashboard login link, where they can edit their business details. Stripe wants contact details, a refund policy and terms on the site by the time the vendor sells, so the vendor terms (A1) are on the path to every vendor's first payment. Read Business website, Express Dashboard.

Steps 2 to 6, decided

Stormed on 31 October 2026, at sticky-note level. How each is built is left to the build.

StepDecided
2An order for a sold-out dish, or for a day closed or ended while the customer was in Checkout, is refused automatically. An order is never partly filled, so one sold-out dish refuses it whole
2, rushThe vendor can pause new orders without closing the day, for a set time with a default on the screen, or until they resume. A timed pause records when it ends and needs no timer; only an open-ended one needs Ordering resumed. An order whose Checkout began before the pause still goes through
